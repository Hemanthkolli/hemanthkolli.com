Why I write Architecture Decision Records

15 Nov 2026 · 2 min read · #architecture #adr

Six months after a decision, nobody remembers why it was made. An Architecture Decision Record (ADR) fixes that: one short note per big decision, saying what we chose and why. Think of it as a ship's log for a software system.

The format I use

# ADR-0007: Use AWS CDK for new platform stacks

## Status
Accepted

## Context
What problem are we solving? What constraints apply?

## Decision
What we chose, in one or two sentences.

## Consequences
What gets easier, what gets harder, what we must watch.
Tip

Keep the records next to the code, for example in docs/adr/, so each decision is reviewed together with the change it explains.

What makes a good ADR

  • One decision per record. If it needs two headings, it's two ADRs.
  • The options you said no to. Often the most useful part.
  • Honest consequences. Every choice has a cost. Write it down.
  • No edits once accepted. If things change, write a new record that replaces the old one.

What it changed for us

ADRs gave us one place to talk through trade-offs before building, and a quick answer when a new teammate asked "why is it like this?". They take minutes to write and save hours of meetings.

If a decision is worth arguing about, it's worth writing down.
